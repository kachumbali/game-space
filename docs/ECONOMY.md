# Economy of the strategic layer: reference review, candidate model, minimal v1 (design proposal)

Status: **research and design proposal, nothing implemented.** Written 2026-10-21 for the owner's request for a persistent
turn-based layer above the dogfight missions (`docs/STRATEGY_LAYER.md`, `docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md`). Fixed facts taken from
`docs/LORE.md`, `docs/SHIP_MANUAL.md`, `docs/CAPITAL_COMBAT.md`, `AGENTS.md`: ships under 500 m; the 450 m frigate
"Exeter" is the only jump-capable class and docks 2 corvettes + 12 fighters/large drones without repairing them; 6 m fighter
drones, 48 per frigate, lost until replenished at a base or by a logistics ship; nuclear warheads rare; PD and lasers use
magazines/capacitors and heat; FROGGs are a migratory hive with neuro-slaved pilots (biology and backstory undecided).

## How to read the evidence tags
- **[V]** verified by fetching the cited page on 2026-10-21. The fetch tool returns a model-generated extract of the page,
  not the page itself. Quotations below come from those extracts and should be re-checked against the page before they are
  cited outside this repo.
- **[M]** recollection, not verified in this session.
- **[PH]** placeholder number of ours, derived from nothing external. Every number in sections 6 and 7 is [PH].
- **[D]** our own design inference.
Pages I tried and could not fetch (Wikipedia "Wing Commander (video game series)", Wikipedia "Children of a Dead Earth",
Starsector fandom "Logistics", Wikipedia Hearts of Iron IV) are not used as evidence.

## Summary of findings
1. **No reference game combines what is asked.** The Wing Commander series and FreeSpace 2 have a war narrative without an
   economy; Panzer General, Battle Isle and Starsector have a persistent force with losses and supply but no pilot-level
   identity beyond experience stars; 4X games have an economy but abstract the fight away. The closest working analogue
   for "persistent force + supplies + battles you may play" is **Starsector** (supplies, fuel, crew, battle-driven cost),
   and the closest for "named people who can die" is **XCOM: Enemy Unknown**. Neither is a space-fighter game, so
   balance numbers cannot be borrowed. [V, section 1]
2. **Recommended resource count is small: Supplies, Fuel, and two non-fungible stocks (Pilots, Nuclear warheads).**
   Separate resources are only worth having if they come from different places or are spent on different things
   [D]. Stellaris lists at least 15 material resources plus abstract ones [V]; that is the opposite of this goal.
3. **Cost should be paid at replenishment, not at loss.** Losses create a *debt ledger* (hull, drones, munitions, repair);
   a depot with finite throughput turns the ledger back into stock. This makes "drones lost until replenished at a
   base" and "docking does not repair" the central logistics constraint with no extra rules [D].
4. **The owner's own constraints reduce the strategic layer to one or two pieces on the board.** Only frigates jump, each
   carries 2 + 12 + 48. With one task force, an economy of five resource types is out of proportion to the number of
   decisions it feeds. Either field 2-4 frigates or keep the economy to the minimum in section 6 (objection O1).
5. **"Capture systems" and "migratory hive" pull in different directions** (objection O2): a hive that eats and leaves
   produces no stable enemy territory to capture. The FROGG side should be modelled as a **clock and a depletion
   process**, not a mirrored economy.
6. **Nothing here can be balanced on paper.** Section 8 lists what to simulate headlessly; the first quantity to measure
   is the real loss distribution of a flown mission, because the whole cost model hangs on it.

## 1. Reference designs and what they teach

### 1.1 Wing Commander series, Privateer
- Wing Commander (1990) used a "campaign tree": the path depends on the player's performance in earlier missions, and
  "newsreel" cinematics reflect success or failure. [V: [Wikipedia, Wing Commander franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_Commander_(franchise))]
- Wing Commander II: the mission tree has "winning" and "losing" paths; the game continues after failed objectives on the
  winning path as long as the protagonist survives, and on the losing path the player must clear those missions to proceed.
  Wingmen "can also no longer be killed during normal gameplay" and eject when destroyed; some die in scripted scenes. [V:
  [Wikipedia, WC II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_Commander_II:_Vengeance_of_the_Kilrathi)]
- Privateer: a sandbox in which "the player must make a profit from price differences of commodities on different planets
  or stations"; mission pay buys better weapons and equipment; non-plot missions are generated by a mission computer;
  faction reactions depend on player actions. [V: [Wikipedia, Privateer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_Commander:_Privateer)]
- Armada is said to have had "both an action and a strategy game mode" but the page gives no detail. [V for the sentence,
  nothing verified about the mechanics.]

Lessons [D from V facts]:
- The war layer in the mainline series is **narrative branching, not an economy**. There is no precedent in it for what the
  owner asks; the owner's layer is a new design, not a revival.
- Soft permanence works commercially: WC II lets wingmen eject and keeps deaths for scripted moments. This is the
  "heroic rescue" end of the permadeath dial in `docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 7.
- Privateer shows that credits-for-equipment with mission pay is enough to make flying meaningful without a strategic map.
  A cheap fallback if the strategic layer stalls: **contract missions that pay Supplies** (section 5.2).
- Trade by price difference does not fit a military campaign with a single convoy-less force; do not import it in v1.

### 1.2 Panzer General and Battle Isle
- Panzer General: scenarios are linked into a campaign; success in battle earns prestige "to upgrade units, acquire
  additional units"; units gain experience and, in campaign mode, "one then has to protect the experienced units as the most
  valuable assets"; campaigns branch and end "either by the general being sacked for incompetence or end of the war". [V:
  [Wikipedia, Panzer General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzer_General)]
- A player guide notes that the defender earns a small fixed prestige per turn per key city held, and that "if you can
  replace losses faster than the enemy destroys them, you win by default"; core forces are bought, e.g. fighters and
  bombers; experienced fighters survive where green ones "get reamed". [V: [StrategyWiki, Panzer General gameplay](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Panzer_General/Gameplay)]
  The page does not describe replacement and supply rules; I did not verify them. The "core unit" concept is
  therefore [M] beyond the guide's use of the word.
- Battle Isle: "Fuel and ammo is limited, and logistics require careful attention"; later games add support units
  (ammo and fuel transports); buildings repair and sometimes produce; experience can in later games be transferred
  over a campaign; events can add objectives, reinforcements, allies or enemies. [V: [Wikipedia, Battle Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Isle)]
  Battle Isle 2200's mechanics were not covered by the page.

Lessons:
- **Persistent force + one global currency (prestige) + experience** is a proven shape for a "battle = a mission" campaign.
  Prestige works because it is the *score of the last battle turned into the strength of the next*. That is exactly the
  coupling the owner wants, with the failure mode already visible: a player who replaces losses faster than they occur
  snowballs (guide statement above).
- Experience makes the unit worth protecting, which is the lever for pilot value (section 4).
- Battle Isle's ammo/fuel plus transports and repair buildings is the template for "depot + logistics ship". Its stated
  evolution (logistics added in later games) suggests the support layer is where complexity creeps; keep it optional.

### 1.3 Master of Orion II, Stellaris, Distant Worlds
- MoO2: colonists are moved between farming, industry and research; food must balance; surplus food is shipped by
  freighters with upkeep; a hostile warship can "blockade an entire system", stopping food and **halving farming and
  industrial output**; ship maintenance cost grows with fleet size, command points cap fleet size and going over makes the
  command rating negative, "very expensive"; "falling behind in technology is likely to be fatal". [V: [Wikipedia, MoO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Orion_II:_Battle_at_Antares)]
- Stellaris: all resources can be stockpiled; each has production and upkeep; stock at zero with negative income triggers
  a shortage; storage overflow is lost; "Material resources are used for most upkeep, from pops to ships to megastructures
  and more"; resource classes are basic (6 listed), advanced (2), strategic (3), rare (4), plus abstract ones. [V:
  [Stellaris wiki, Resources](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Resources)]
  The fetch covered the first 100,000 of 142,276 characters; fleet-upkeep and starbase sections were not read.
- Distant Worlds: the page gives scale (up to 1,400 systems, 50,000 bodies) and states it has task-automation systems; it
  says nothing on resources or logistics. [V: [Wikipedia, Distant Worlds: Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distant_Worlds:_Universe)]

Lessons:
- Blockade as "output halved, supply stopped" is a ready, legible raid mechanic (section 3.4).
- Upkeep that grows with fleet size is the standard anti-snowball valve; MoO2's version is a hard cap plus penalty.
- Stellaris is a counter-example for legibility. Distant Worlds' automation is a symptom of scale: at 5-12 systems, no
  automation layer should be needed; if one is, the model is too big.

### 1.4 Hearts of Iron supply
- HoI4 (wiki, version note 1.11+): supplies are **not produced**; a province has a *supply capacity* that limits how many
  units it can sustain, from state supply, hub supply (rail, ports, convoys) and air supply; capacity along a link is
  limited by its weakest segment; hub supply falls off with province distance; captured rail is unusable for a cooldown
  (10 days in non-core territory, 5 in core); units at or below 35% supply take attrition; cut-off divisions get no
  manpower or equipment reinforcements; convoy routes that are raided supply less until they recover; naval units rarely
  suffer undersupply. [V: [HoI4 wiki, Supply](https://hoi4.paradoxwikis.com/Supply)]

Lessons:
- **Supply as capacity, not stock** is an alternative design to stockpiles: a node supports N ships, falling with
  distance. It is cleaner for continuous front lines than for our case, where losses must be *replaced* (a stock event).
  A hybrid is proposed in 3.2: connectedness is capacity-like (binary), replacement is stock-like.
- "Cut off => no reinforcements, attrition" is a compact, legible penalty. The captured-rail cooldown is a reusable idea:
  a captured depot is offline for N turns.

### 1.5 FreeSpace 2 campaign persistence
- FS2's campaign is linear ("without any branching paths"), with optional covert missions; after five consecutive
  failures a mission may be skipped; ship and weapon choices grow as the campaign proceeds; the page reports no pilot
  or ship persistence in single player. [V: [Wikipedia, FreeSpace 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeSpace_2)]
- Our own source study of the SCP codebase reports a graph with branches, persistent goals/events/variables and
  per-mission "red alert" ship state (`docs/FS2OPEN_STUDY.md` section 5; read from a repo clone, not re-verified here).
  There is no economy in either.

Lessons: persistence of *variables* (flags, counters) is cheap and supports narrative. "Skip after repeated failure" is an
accepted anti-grind valve in this genre; it should exist as **retreat/abandon** in our layer. Unlocks by campaign progress
is the FS2 substitute for a tech economy; research could be replaced by it in v1.

### 1.6 X series, Elite
- X3: prices rise as stock falls; factories consume power and resources and stop if supply exceeds demand; trading
  stations hold stock that depletes; NPC traders hunt best routes; players can build factories and link them; the page
  rates the economy "the most advanced, realistic, and arguably the most competitive" of the X games. [V:
  [Wikipedia, X3: Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X3:_Reunion)]
- Elite Dangerous: a "background simulation" changes the galaxy; Powerplay (2015) is a "grand strategy layer" whose
  outcome follows the collective achievements of players; community goals produce persistent changes in systems; the
  map is a 1:1 Milky Way. [V: [Wikipedia, Elite Dangerous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Dangerous)]

Lessons: these are **player-embedded simulations of many agents**, built for open-world trade and tens of thousands of
systems. Their cost is simulation depth and server-scale content. Neither suggests how to run a closed military campaign
with a handful of units. Take only: a *background clock* that changes systems whether or not you act (Elite), and
input-output chains as a late-game option, not v1.

### 1.7 Starsector (logistics, supplies, crew)
Included because it is the nearest working design to "persistent fleet, supplies, battles whose cost is the point".
- Every ship consumes a set amount of supplies each month; restoring combat readiness (CR) costs supplies; with no
  supplies CR drains; "Battles are the main expense, for deployment and for replacing CR"; recommended practice is to
  carry about 100 times the daily maintenance before undocking (200 for combat); mothballing cuts a ship's maintenance by
  90%; cargo, fuel or crew over capacity incurs an ongoing supply cost; fuel is a separate commodity. [V:
  [Starsector wiki, Supplies](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Supplies), [Economy](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Economy)]
- Crew: each ship has a skeleton crew needed for 70% CR; "some crew are lost every time a fighter is destroyed"; crew
  have a monthly salary and leave if unpaid; the page does not describe a recruitment mechanic. [V: [Starsector wiki, Crew](https://starsector.wiki.gg/wiki/Crew)]
- Developer's 2013 reasoning: logistics reduced to "a single number" so that accidents have "just one cause to watch for";
  fleet size and logistics decoupled. [V: [Fractal Softworks blog, 2013](https://fractalsoftworks.com/2013/05/25/logistics-fleet-management)]
  This is a 2013 post; the live game has changed since (the wiki pages above describe later systems).

Lessons:
- **Three-way split supplies / fuel / crew** is a verified, playable decomposition; a "single logistics number" is an
  intentional legibility choice.
- Cost is dominated by **deploying and recovering from battle**, which is the property we want: fights cost, and the
  player decides how often to fight.
- Idle upkeep reduction (mothball) is a cheap valve against upkeep death spirals; consider "reserve" status for hulls.

### 1.8 XCOM: Enemy Unknown (for the people layer)
- Soldiers "may permanently die once their health reaches zero if they are not saved in time"; the designers used
  permadeath for tension; soldiers gain experience and promotions; classes are assigned randomly; funding nations that
  leave never rejoin; panic depends on which situations the player responds to; base management has construction and
  research. [V: [Wikipedia, XCOM: Enemy Unknown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XCOM:_Enemy_Unknown)]

Lessons: a *rescue window* ("if not saved in time") is a known permadeath softener and matches the ADRIFT state in
`docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md`. The "ignore a region and panic rises" loop is a compact way to make **not being everywhere** costly
without simulating logistics; it is also the standard way a strategic layer pressures the player into fighting.

### 1.9 Children of a Dead Earth (mass, power, heat budgets)
- Its store page claims each system's "efficiency, size, mass, power usage, heat dissipation, are all derived from valid
  equations"; ships are freeform builds of rockets, tanks, weapons, powerplants, radiators, crew modules, armour;
  delta-v and moment of inertia are calculated; there is a campaign as an admiral and a design sandbox. [V:
  [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/476530/Children_of_a_Dead_Earth/)] The page excerpt describes no
  cost or economy system; whether the game has one I could not verify.

Lesson [D]: it is a **design-method** reference, not an economy reference. Mass, power and heat are the budgets; money is
not. For us that suggests deriving class costs from a physical proxy (hull length or mass) and keeping the ratios fixed,
so that "a frigate is worth N fighters" is stated once. Our own repo already uses this approach for nuclear
parameters (`docs/CAPITAL_COMBAT.md` section 2).

### 1.10 Space: Above and Beyond (fiction, not mechanics)
- Premise: Earth fights the Chigs (2063-64); colonisation depends on "transient but predictable, naturally occurring
  wormholes" that constrain travel and supply; the main fleets suffer heavy losses; one episode shows Earth "forced to
  abandon 25,000 stranded troops" to launch another assault; In Vitroes are genetically engineered soldiers "bred to replace
  losses" in the Silicate war; the Marine fighter squadron serves as pilots and infantry on a carrier. The page says little
  about the wider war economy. [V: [Wikipedia, Space: Above and Beyond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space:_Above_and_Beyond)]

Lessons [D]: (a) **chokepoint mobility sets logistics**, close to our "only frigates jump" rule; the fiction treats it as a
constraint that generates drama (stranded forces). (b) Manpower is both replaceable (In Vitro) and morally costly; a
"fast but lower-quality recruit" option is a story-compatible lever (section 4). (c) The series never explains a
production base; the economy there is background, which supports keeping ours thin.

### 1.11 Independence Day (for the FROGG framing)
- The film's aliens plan to harvest Earth's resources "as they have done to other civilizations"; saucers launch shielded
  fighters; the saucers are described as about 24 km in diameter. [V: [Wikipedia, Independence Day (1996 film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_(1996_film))]
  The extract did not describe locust-like migration or planet hopping explicitly; that reading is [M].

Lesson: "harvest and move on" is a **depletion clock** from the human side's point of view. It leaves room for a game
where the enemy is not a mirrored economy but a pressure with a visible rate.

### 1.12 Lanchester's laws (for auto-resolve, not for economy)
- Linear law: melee and unaimed area fire; square law: aimed fire at range, "the rate of attrition now depends only on the
  number of weapons shooting", power grows with the square of numbers; the article states the model does not cover
  machine guns, unguided artillery or nuclear weapons. [V: [Wikipedia, Lanchester's laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanchester%27s_laws)]

Lesson: a Lanchester-style auto-resolve is a defensible *starting* form, but missile saturation versus PD and nukes fall
outside it, so its constants must be fitted to measured mission outcomes (section 8).

## 2. Candidate resource model for a small, legible game

### 2.1 Selection rule
Add a resource only if it (a) comes from different places than the others, or (b) is spent on a different decision
[D]. Stellaris-style breadth fails this test; Starsector's supplies/fuel/crew split passes it.

### 2.2 Candidates and verdict
| Resource | Sources | Sinks | Verdict for v1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supplies (S)**: industrial output; stands for hull materials, drone stock, missiles, spares | industrial worlds, belts, salvage, contracts | replacement of losses, rearm, repair, upkeep | **Keep.** Single pool for materiel and munitions: both come from the same industry [D] |
| **Fuel (F)**: reaction mass and jump-energy feedstock | gas giants, ice moons, comets, fuel depots | frigate jumps, long transits | **Keep**, but only if the map has refuelling distance constraints; otherwise it is dead weight (see example, section 7) |
| **Pilots (P)**: roster, not a pool | population centres via recruits | trained over turns; lost to KIA/capture | **Keep as roster characters**, with a small recruit flow (section 4) |
| **Nuclear warheads (N)**: integer stock | not produced; fixed starting stock, rare finds, story rewards | used one at a time on heavy missiles | **Keep as a stock with no production**, because "rare, limited" is the owner's rule |
| Drones | derived from S at depots | replenished at S cost, throughput-limited | **Not a resource**; a unit class with a stock per frigate |
| Munitions (missiles, torpedoes, PD reloads) | derived from S | rearm at depot | **Fold into S**; keep per-round costs for the loss ledger |
| Power / jump energy | frigate reactor | per jump | **Fold into F**: one jump = fixed F; power for PD/lasers stays inside missions (`docs/CAPITAL_COMBAT.md`) |
| Research | labs | tech | **Defer.** Unlocks come from campaign progress as in FS2 (1.5) until there is something to research |
| Intel | scouts, drone scouting | reveals enemy strength, unlocks special objectives (`docs/STRATEGY_LAYER.md`) | **Not a resource**; a per-system visibility state, earned by scouting actions (drone "scout ahead" is a natural source) |
| Money / trade | markets | purchases | **Cut.** Privateer/X-style trade fits a different game (1.1, 1.6) |
| Population / morale | worlds | recruits, stability | **Defer.** Morale is a likely trap: another meter to read |

### 2.3 Where resources come from
| System type | Yield | Special |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial world | S, high | can host a Base or Shipyard |
| Colony / population centre | S low, recruits | trains pilots |
| Asteroid belt | S medium | no population; easy to raid [D] |
| Gas giant / ice moon | F | refuelling point |
| Frontier / dead world | low or none | strategic value only (lane, depot position) |
Derived rule: **yield depends on control state** (held, contested, blockaded, lost), see 3.4.

### 2.4 How battle losses become economic cost
Use a **loss ledger** kept per task force and settled at a depot:

| Loss | Where it is paid | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hull destroyed | queue at shipyard, S, build time | frigate and corvettes take many turns |
| Hull damaged | repair at a Base: `repair_factor x build cost x fraction damaged` | Starsector has the same shape: repair costs supplies and is the main post-battle expense [V, 1.7] |
| Drones lost | depot, S per drone, throughput cap per turn | the throughput cap is the lever that makes consecutive battles costly |
| Missiles, PD reloads | depot, S | trivial per round, large per big battle |
| Pilot | roster state machine (section 4) | no S cost to lose; recruiting and training costs time |
| Nuclear warhead | stock decrement | not replaceable |
| Prisoners, salvage | credit S from wrecks (`disable/capture`, `docs/STRATEGY_LAYER.md`) | optional faucet; prevents pure attrition death spirals |
Because cost is paid **later and at a limited-throughput place**, a victorious but costly battle can still leave the
player unable to fight the next one for several turns. This is the intended tempo mechanism [D].

## 3. Logistics and geography

### 3.1 Mobility
Only frigates jump (owner rule). A task force (TF) is therefore a frigate plus up to 2 corvettes and 12 fighters/large
drones, and the 48 stored 6 m drones. Consequences for the economy [D]:
- **The frigate is the economy's critical asset**: loss of one frigate strands everything docked in it (rule in
  `docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 8) and removes mobility. Its cost relative to fighters must make this feel catastrophic
  but survivable (section 6 sets 75 fighter-equivalents [PH]).
- **Slot economics.** The frigate has two corvette cradles. A logistics ship that is a corvette-class hull occupies a
  cradle: a TF either brings a second warship or a mobile depot. If the logistics ship is smaller than a corvette, it
  competes for fighter slots. The brief does not say which; this is an open question (section 9).
- **Control capacity.** Drones per controller: fighter 1, heavy fighter 2, corvette 6. A full TF of 12 light fighters and
  2 corvettes controls at most 12 + 12 = 24 drones, up to 36 with heavy fighters in place of light ones; 48 stored drones
  are therefore a **reserve of 12-24 above what can be slaved**. That is a reasonable margin; it also means the number 48
  is not binding unless losses are heavy.
- **Range.** Fuel per jump and a frigate tank limit how far from a refuelling point a TF can operate (section 6).

### 3.2 Supply lines, depots and connectedness (hybrid of HoI4 and stockpiles)
- Each system has a *connection state* to the capital: **connected** if a chain of friendly-held, un-blockaded systems
  links it by lanes. This is a binary version of HoI4 hub reach [V, 1.4].
- A connected system's output adds to the global pool each turn. An unconnected system's output accumulates in a **local
  stockpile** up to a cap; a TF present there may use it. This costs one integer per system, needs no convoy units,
  and makes cutting a lane a real act [D].
- **Depots** (levels 1-3, section 6.3) are the only places to rearm, replenish drones, repair, recover pilots. Without
  convoy units, "supply line" means *the chain of systems*, and "logistics ship" means a mobile level-1 depot with a
  stock limit and a jump-capable carrier.
- Pure HoI4 capacity (no stock) was rejected for v1: losses must be *purchased back*, which is a stock event [D].

### 3.3 Territory to income
Income = sum over connected systems of `yield x control factor`. Control factors [PH]: held 1.0, contested 0.5, blockaded
0.5, enemy-held 0, depleted by FROGGs as in 3.5. Upkeep grows with fleet size and, optionally, number of systems held
(MoO2 analogue [V, 1.3]) to limit snowballing (section 5).

### 3.4 Blockade and raid
- **Blockade:** an enemy armed presence in a system with no defending TF halves its output and breaks connectedness
  through it (MoO2 blockade [V, 1.3]; HoI4 cut-off penalties [V, 1.4]).
- **Raid (small enemy group):** targets a depot or a lane node; effect: delay (offline N turns, HoI4 captured-rail
  cooldown idea [V, 1.4]) and a fraction of local stockpile destroyed. The raid is the usual **mission generator**: it
  produces defend-the-depot or intercept missions, which are dogfights.
- **Human raids on FROGGs:** FROGGs have no supply chain in the human sense (section 3.5), so human raiding targets brood
  strength and appetite (carriers, brood nests), not logistics.

### 3.5 FROGGs as an asymmetric economy
Owner premise: migratory hive that consumes systems and relocates. A model that fits it with three numbers [D]:
- **Brood fleets** with a strength rating. Each brood occupies one system.
- **Depletion** of the occupied system: each turn its yield multiplier drops (e.g. x0.5 per turn, [PH]) until barren.
  Consumed yield becomes **Biomass (B)**, a hidden FROGG-side counter.
- **Growth and relocation:** when B passes a threshold, the brood spawns another brood or reinforces itself; when the
  system is depleted below a threshold, the brood moves to the best adjacent target (highest remaining yield weighted by
  lowest defence). The human side sees two things only: *where* the broods are and an *appetite/threat* indication.
Properties:
- Human extraction does not deplete systems in v1; FROGG presence does. This is the asymmetry; it is also a rule that
  makes "hold" and "contest" matter more than "expand".
- The enemy has no costs to balance: strength comes from B and from scripted phases of the campaign. That cuts a whole
  simulation (the FROGG production and logistics tree) which would add nothing the player can see.
- FROGG pilots cannot be rescued (`docs/LORE.md`): their losses cost B, not roster. Human nuclear stock being effective
  against radiation-vulnerable pilots (`docs/CAPITAL_COMBAT.md`) means an N is worth more against dense broods; the
  mission generator may need to produce nuke-appropriate targets so the stock is spendable.
- **Hive motherships and carriers (1.5-10 km) are deferred**; v1 brood fleets are made of fighters, Gulper corvettes and,
  later, the planned FROGG frigate. A brood's "centre of gravity" target in a mission can be the corvette or frigate
  in the 100-500 m class.

## 4. Pilot and crew economy

### 4.1 Finite roster, pipeline
Pilot lifecycle (extends `docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 4):
`RECRUIT -> TRAINEE (t turns at a Base) -> ACTIVE -> (eject) ADRIFT -> RESCUED -> RECOVERING (n turns) -> ACTIVE`, or
`KIA` / `CAPTURED`. [PH rates in 6.5.]
- **Recruit flow** comes from population systems, a slow trickle, so roster size is a *stock the player manages*, not a
  currency. Starting roster ~ one pilot per fighter slot plus a small reserve (about 14 for a 12-fighter TF [PH]).
- **Trainee quality.** A rushed recruit (shorter training, lower skill) is the In Vitro analogue [V, 1.10] and offers an
  optional emergency valve, at the cost of a worse starting skill and traits.
- **Veteran value.** Panzer General's experience stars are the verified model of "protect the experienced units" [V, 1.2].
  Pilot experience should improve (a) the AI wingman skill when the pilot is not the player's, and (b) the player-flown
  pilot only through small transparent bonuses (`docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 4). The economic point is that **a
  veteran's replacement cost is time, not S**, and cannot be bought.
- **Ejection and SAR.** Rescue odds: `p_eject x p_rescue`, where `p_rescue` depends on control of the system and SAR capacity
  in the TF (`docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 5). With the placeholders in 6.5, about 42% of crewed-fighter losses end with the
  pilot recovered; the rest are KIA or captured. This is a tuning dial, not a derived number.
- **Heavy fighters (pilot + weapons specialist).** Two persistent characters per sortie, one pod, both rescued or both
  lost (owner answers, `docs/FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 9a). Economic consequences:
  - Specialists form a second roster pool with its own recruit and training cost; if both pools are fed by the same
    population source, specialist supply is the binding constraint on heavy fighters [D].
  - Because specialists run 2 drones and the ECM/munitions seat, a heavy fighter adds control capacity (+2 drones) at the
    price of two people. Heavy fighters should therefore be limited by specialists, not by S.
  - The specialist seat is AI-driven for now; the persistent character still exists. Their experience can feed the
    quality of ECM/munitions management (an AI parameter), a natural place for a visible veteran bonus.
- **Drone kills** accrue to the controller (owner rule) and count towards controller experience separately.

### 4.2 Permadeath and session length
XCOM's permadeath is a design pillar [V, 1.8]; WC II's wingmen are almost unkillable by design [V, 1.1]. The owner's
`FLEET_LOGIC.md` already flags the mismatch with 5-minute sessions. Dial: permadeath on/off, rescue window length,
`p_eject`. A key observation [D]: **the roster is the one resource the player cannot buy back with S**, so it carries
the emotional weight; the economy should not undercut it by making pilots cheap to replace.

## 5. Campaign structure options and failure modes

### 5.1 Options
| Option | Shape | Strength | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Fixed-length scenario | 25-40 turns, 5-8 systems, lose by capital falling, win by clock or objective | testable, no runaway | feels scripted; replay value depends on enemy randomness |
| B. Narrative tree on top | WC/FS2 style branches decided by outcomes of key battles; economy modulates which forces you bring | strong story fit, cheap | economy becomes decoration if branches decide everything |
| C. Open-ended sandbox | expand until FROGGs are driven off | long-term play | grind, runaway leader, spreadsheet fatigue (Stellaris pattern [V, 1.3]) |
| D. Roguelite runs | short randomised map, persistent roster only across runs | fits phone sessions | persistence across runs conflicts with "persistent roster of named characters with memories" unless the roster is the meta-progression |
Recommendation [D]: **A with B-style authored beats**, 8-12 systems at most (the existing proposal), v1 on 5.

### 5.2 Failure modes and mitigations
| Failure mode | Cause | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Grind** | repeated fights to farm S | S from fights only as salvage with a per-system cap; contract missions have finite number; "retreat/abandon" valve like FS2's skip after repeated failure [V, 1.5] |
| **Runaway leader** | replacing losses faster than the enemy kills them [V, 1.2], income growing with territory | upkeep grows with fleet and holdings (MoO2 command rating [V, 1.3]); FROGG pressure scales with *time*, not with human losses, so winning early does not shrink the threat |
| **Death spiral** | frigate lost => no mobility => income collapses | reserve status (mothball analogue, Starsector 90% [V, 1.7]); capital always has a fallback: the shipyard can rebuild a minimum-viable TF at a fixed S discount; scenario rule "second frigate in reserve" |
| **Spreadsheet fatigue** | more than ~3 flows to track | resource limit in section 2; one screen showing S, F, N, pilots; ledger summary line rather than detail |
| **Nuke hoarding** | decisive weapon, limited stock, no deadline [M: this is a common pattern with limited super-weapons] | targets that justify use are generated at fixed points; stock can be captured or destroyed with the carrier; or warheads have a shelf life. Needs playtest |
| **Auto-resolve dominance** | players skip the dogfight | expected auto-resolve result is *worse* than the average flown result and loses more of the irreplaceable assets (pilots, N); flown missions give special rewards (rescue, capture, Intel) as in `docs/STRATEGY_LAYER.md` |
| **Variance from flown results** | a lucky or unlucky player flight decides the campaign | cap the economic swing of a single battle; give a "recover" action; do not hide FROGG strength once telegraphed |
| **Save-scumming** | strategic state is JSON | store the RNG seed in the save; decide whether export/import is "restore point" or "cheat" |

### 5.3 Keeping the dogfight the core
The economy should reach the dogfight only through [D]:
1. **Composition.** What you bring: how many fighters, drones, heavy fighters, whether a corvette or a tender, nukes.
2. **Pilot.** Who flies, with small transparent bonuses.
3. **Objective type.** Generated from strategic situation: defend depot, intercept raid, escort refit convoy, recover pods, strike carrier.
4. **Consequence.** The mission result writes a ledger and roster states; nothing else changes.
The economy should *not* feed numbers into the fighter's flight model (no stat inflation for the player's craft). That would
make the strategic layer a second game that overrides the first.

## 6. Recommended minimal v1 model (all numbers are [PH])

### 6.1 Resources and state
- **Supplies (S)**, **Fuel (F)** global pools; per-system local stockpile only when disconnected.
- **Nuclear warheads (N)** integer, no production.
- **Pilots** roster with states (section 4), recruit counter.
- **Ledger** per TF: hull damage, drones lost, rounds expended, pending replacements.
- **FROGG state**: broods (location, strength), per-system depletion level, hidden B.

### 6.2 System types and yields (per turn, connected, held)
| Type | S | F | Recruits | Feature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home / capital | 12 | 1 | 0 | Depot L3 (rearm, drones, repair, build, pilot recovery) |
| Colony | 6 | 0 | 1 per 2 turns | Depot L1 (rearm, drones; throughput 6 S/turn) |
| Fuel moon | 0 | 3 | 0 | refuel |
| Belt | 5 | 0 | 0 | no defence value |
| Frontier | 4 | 0 | 0 | lane position |
Depot throughput per turn [PH]: L1 6 S, L2 15 S (adds repair), L3 30 S (adds build and queue).

### 6.3 Costs per class and unit (S unless stated)
| Item | Build cost | Build time | Upkeep/turn | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drone (6 m) | 0.5 | at depot, within throughput | 0 | pure replacement cost; frigate stores 48 |
| Fighter (Kestrel class) | 4 | 2 turns | 0.1 | needs a pilot from roster |
| Heavy fighter | 8 | 3 turns | 0.2 | needs pilot + specialist |
| Corvette | 40 | 5 turns | 1 | cradle slot |
| Frigate (Exeter) | 300 | 12 turns | 3 | the only jumper; 75 light fighters in cost [PH] |
| Standard missile | 0.2 | rearm at depot | - | |
| Heavy missile / torpedo | 1 | rearm at depot | - | |
| PD magazine full reload | corvette 0.5, frigate 1 | rearm | - | |
| Nuclear warhead | not buildable | - | - | stock only; for scoring treat as 30 S |
| Repair | 20% of build cost x fraction hull lost | at L2+ | - | |
| Jump | 2 F per lane-length unit | 1 turn per jump | - | frigate tank 12 F; refuel at fuel moon or any depot |
Cost ratios are a design choice, not derived: hull length ratio 12:128:450 m (about 1:11:38) maps to cost ratio 4:40:300
(1:10:75), i.e. super-linear in length but far below volume ratio. The choice makes the frigate expensive in a way the
player will care about without making it unreplaceable over a 30-turn campaign [D].

### 6.4 Turn structure
1. **Logistics** (at depots, within throughput): rearm, replenish drones, repair, build queue, refuel.
2. **Orders**: each TF chooses move/jump, hold, blockade, SAR sweep, scout, or reserve.
3. **Battles**: for each contested system, fly or auto-resolve; apply ledger and roster changes.
4. **Economy**: income and upkeep. Ledger settlement is not here; it is paid at a depot in step 1 of the next turn.
5. **FROGG phase**: broods deplete, grow, move; telegraph next target to the player (one turn visibility) [D].
Turn abstraction: an operational cycle, not a calendar unit; avoids fixing travel time in game units.

### 6.5 Pilot placeholders
`p_eject` 0.60 on destruction; `p_rescue` 0.70 if the system is held and a SAR-capable asset is present, 0.30 if held
without SAR, 0 otherwise; RECOVERING 2 turns; training 2 turns; recruits 1 per 2 turns per Colony. Expected recovery
per crewed-fighter loss: 0.60 x 0.70 = 0.42.

### 6.6 Starting map (5 systems)
| Id | Name | Type | Start owner | Lanes (length) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | Anchorage | Home | human | K (1), V (1) |
| K | Cinder | Fuel moon | human | H (1), M (2) |
| V | Vale | Colony (Depot L1) | human | H (1), M (1) |
| M | Marrow | Belt | none | K (2), V (1), E (1) |
| E | Ember | Frontier | none, FROGG approaching | M (1) |
Start: S 40, F 10, N 3. TF-1 at H: Exeter (tank 12/12), 2 corvettes, 12 fighters, 48 drones, 14 pilots (12 assigned).

## 7. Worked example, two turns (placeholder numbers, assumed battle outcome)

**Telegraph at start of turn 1:** a FROGG raid group (8 fighters, 1 corvette) will reach V at the end of turn 1; a main
brood is at E and will reach M in about 3 turns (turn 4).

### Turn 1
1. Logistics: nothing to do, everything full.
2. Orders: TF-1 jumps H -> V (1 lane, 2 F from tank: 12 -> 10).
3. Battle at V, flown by the player. Assumed outcome of the flown mission (not a prediction): enemy raid destroyed;
   human losses are 2 fighters (pilot P-07 killed, pilot P-03 ejected), 12 drones, 5 standard missiles, 1 PD reload, frigate
   hull 10% damaged.
4. Economy: income S 18 (H 12 + V 6), F 4 (H 1 + K 3). Upkeep after losses: frigate 3 + corvettes 2 + 10 fighters 1.0 = 6.0.
   S: 40 + 18 - 6.0 = **52.0**. F: 10 + 4 = **14**. Tank 10/12.
5. Ledger (unpaid): 2 fighters 8.0; 12 drones 6.0; 5 missiles 1.0; PD 1.0; frigate repair 0.2 x 300 x 0.10 = 6.0.
   **Total 22.0 S**, which is almost two turns of net income (+12.0 per turn here) for one fight. That is a plausible tempo
   (a flown battle roughly every second turn is affordable), but it is the first number to test (section 8).
6. Pilots: P-07 KIA. P-03 ADRIFT; V is held and the TF has a frigate, so `p_rescue` applies.

### Turn 2
1. Logistics at V (Depot L1, throughput 6 S): rearm 2.0 S (missiles + PD), then 8 drones at 0.5 S = 4.0 S; 4 drones remain
   short. Fighter replacement and hull repair are not possible at V (L1). Refuel 2 F. S: 52.0 - 6.0 = 46.0; F: 14 - 2 = 12; tank 12/12.
   SAR roll for P-03 (assumed success): RECOVERING for 2 turns.
2. Orders: TF-1 jumps V -> M (1 lane, 2 F: tank 10/12), to claim the belt before the brood arrives. Alternative
   (not taken): return to H for repair and fighter replacement, at the cost of M being claimed later or never.
3. No battle. M is claimed at the end of the turn if no enemy is present.
4. Economy: income S 18, F 4 (M yields from turn 3). Upkeep 6.0. S: 46.0 + 18 - 6.0 = **58.0**. F: 12 + 4 = **16**.
5. FROGG phase: the brood at E depletes E (x0.5 yield; E was unowned, so no human income lost), B grows, the brood's next
   target (M) is telegraphed for turn 4.

State at end of turn 2:
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| S / F / N | 58.0 / 16 / 3 |
| TF-1 | frigate hull 90%; 10 fighters; 44 drones; tank 10/12; at M |
| Debt ledger remaining | 2 fighters 8.0 (H only), repair 6.0 (H only), 4 drones 2.0 |
| Pilots | 13 alive (P-07 dead); P-03 recovering; 10 flying, 2 reserve |
| Decision pending | stay at M and fight the brood at turn 4 with a damaged frigate and 4 missing drones, or withdraw to H and give up M |
Observations this example already shows [D]:
- Fuel is non-binding with one TF (F rises, not constrained). Either fuel yield should drop, jump cost should rise, or
  the map should be larger. This is a finding about the model, not an accident of the placeholders.
- The decisions per turn are few (jump? stay? replace?), so the economy mostly answers one question: *how many fights can
  I afford before I must go home*. That is acceptable only if the map offers more than one such choice per turn (more TFs).
- The depot throughput cap, not S stock, is the binding limiter on drone replenishment at V.

## 8. What to simulate headlessly to balance it

Order matters: the first run decides whether the rest is meaningful.
1. **Measure the real loss distribution of a flown mission.** Use the existing headless harness (`AGENTS.md`: stepped
   `__game.update(dt)`, paused loop, Playwright). For a grid of compositions (fighters, drones, corvette count, frigate PD
   state) against a grid of FROGG compositions, record: ship losses, drones lost, rounds expended, hull damage, pilot
   ejections. Whether a bot can fly the player role is unverified. If not, use AI-flown player slots as a proxy and flag it.
   Output: distributions, not means. This is the missing data for the ledger.
2. **Fit an auto-resolve model** (Lanchester-style as a baseline, section 1.12) to those results; report residuals by
   composition. Requirement: expected auto-resolve losses are worse than the flown mean on pilots and N, as agreed in 5.2.
3. **Strategic Monte Carlo**, in pure JS or Python without rendering, with scripted policies: aggressive, turtle, greedy
   expansion, minimum-risk, nuke-hoarder, and a random policy. Metrics: win rate and turn of loss, time series of S, F, N;
   probability of frigate loss by turn; fraction of runs hitting a death spiral (S stock below upkeep for 3 turns);
   fights per run; pilot attrition curve and roster size over time; Gini of resource share across systems; share of
   income from the most valuable system.
4. **Sensitivity sweeps** on the least-grounded parameters: cost ratios, depot throughput, upkeep, `p_eject`, `p_rescue`,
   FROGG depletion rate and B threshold. Report which parameters change win rate by more than 10 points.
5. **Exploit hunt:** policies that avoid all fights, camp at home, or farm one system; verify none dominates.
6. **Enemy telegraph test:** with perfect information vs one-turn telegraph; check that visible information does not make the
   scenario trivially solvable.
7. **Reproducibility:** fixed seeds, every run's config and git commit logged, so results can be re-run after tuning
   (consistent with the repo's testing notes in `AGENTS.md`).
Not testable headless: whether flying is fun and whether the ledger feels fair. That needs the playtest called for in
`docs/STRATEGY_LAYER.md` section 6.

## 9. Open questions for the owner
1. **How many frigates does the player command?** One makes the map a corridor; 2-4 makes the economy matter. This is the
   most consequential unknown.
2. **Logistics ship size class.** Corvette-sized (takes a cradle), fighter-sized (takes slots), or non-docking
   civilian that cannot enter hostile systems alone? Does it have a jump drive (only frigates do)?
3. **Drone storage vs docking.** The brief lists "12 fighters/large fighter drones" docked and "48 drones" stored. Do the 6 m
   drones count toward the 12 slots? Does a larger drone exist at all, or is "large fighter drone" a different class?
4. **Is the player the commander of all TFs, or one flight leader?** `STRATEGY_LAYER.md` question 1 is still open in
   effect: it decides whether to treat the economy as player-owned or simply as a scoreboard.
5. **What does the player win?** Destroy the brood, outlast the migration, evacuate? Each yields a different economy.
6. **Are human extraction sites finite?** Finite deposits would create real stakes for the human side too; I propose no for v1.
7. **How should unfought battles resolve?** Auto-resolve is assumed. Is there a "reinforce from another TF" rule?
8. **Permadeath default** and rescue window; and whether recruits are a flow or fixed.
9. **Where do nuclear warheads come from in the story?** Rare finds, allied transfers, or an old stockpile? This decides how
   the player experiences the stock.
10. **Are human factions (deferred) distinct economically?** If they trade or compete, the economy will need a diplomacy layer.
11. **Save policy.** Export/import allowed? Seed stored?

## 10. Objections to the owner's assumptions
- **O1. The owner's scale constraints make the strategic piece count too small for a rich economy.** One jump-capable
  frigate with fixed docking is a "single piece" per player. Economic depth buys little unless there are more TFs, more
  decisions per turn, or more reasons to be in different places. Counter-argument I considered: Starsector runs a single fleet
  with a rich economy [V, 1.7]; but its fleet is variable in composition, which ours is not (12 + 2 fixed per frigate).
- **O2. "Systems captured" vs "migratory hive".** If the hive eats and leaves, territory is a transient, not a prize. If
  the human side captures systems, the contest is territorial and the hive must stay and defend. The two framings need
  one decision. My v1 resolves by making the hive's output a *depletion process* and the human gain "holding what is left".
- **O3. "Rare nuclear" plus "FROGG radiation-vulnerable" creates an elixir problem** [M]. A weapon that is decisive,
  limited and without a clear use-case tends not to be used. Not a reason to remove the rule, but it has to be
  designed with explicit targets or the stock will sit unused.
- **O4. Persistent named roster + "player chooses a pilot" assumes the pilot choice has consequences.** If experience
  bonuses are small (as in `FLEET_LOGIC.md`), the economy of pilots is mostly narrative. That is acceptable but should be
  admitted; otherwise the roster is a presentation layer over an S economy.
- **O5. Drone economics.** At 0.5 S per drone versus a pilot's irreplaceable time, drones will be the rational first spend
  and crewed fighters become a cost-ineffective choice unless drones are tightly bounded by control limits and ECM/jamming
  rules. The jamming rules are deferred; until they exist, the drone/pilot balance cannot be assessed.
- **O6. "Docking does not repair" and "drones lost until replenished at a base" are strong constraints.** They are sufficient to make
  logistics matter. Adding fuel, upkeep and munitions as separate flows on top may be redundant. I kept fuel because jump
  range is the only thing that makes multi-system geography matter; if the first sim shows fuel never binds (as the
  example does), drop it.
- **O7. Time and "turn" semantics.** Telling fiction time apart from turn time matters for SAR life-support timers
  (`FLEET_LOGIC.md` section 5): a pod's endurance has to be expressed in turns.
- **O8. Evidence limits.** The reference review shows what other designs do, not what works for this one. Several
  checked games are decades old and described by short encyclopedia articles; the economics of Privateer, X3 and Elite
  are verified only at the sentence level. No claim in this document is validated by play.

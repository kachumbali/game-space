# Human Factions & Strategic Landscape

Humanity's expansion across the Sol Sector relies on hollowed, spun-up asteroid habitats rather than lush planetary colonies. With Earth and only two terrestrial worlds ("Garden Worlds") available, 85% of humans live in artificial low-gravity habitats. Political unity does not exist.

## 1. Earth-Core Union (ECU) / United Terrestrial Defense Force (UTDF)
- **Territory:** Earth, Luna, and the two Garden Worlds (New Caledonia, Aurelia).
- **Culture:** Bureaucratic, wealthy, and culturally dominant. ECU leadership views off-world station dwellers as secondary labor units.
- **Military Doctrine:** Heavy composite armor, standardized kinetic cannons, rapid-fire miniguns, and layered point-defense (CRAM-style guns and PD-30 lasers).
- **Primary Line Craft:** Kestrel (single-seat fighter) and Halberd (two-seat heavy fighter).

## 2. Void-Born Confederacy (VBC)
- **Territory:** The outer asteroid belts and Oort cloud industrial clusters.
- **Infrastructure:** Sidero-Nodes ("Hollows")—5 to 20 km nickel-iron asteroids hollowed out with fusion torches, sealed, and spun to generate 0.3g–0.8g gravity along the inner cylinder walls. Launches occur via zero-g axial tubes running down the asteroid's central spin axis.
- **Culture:** Resource-starved, pragmatic, and highly self-reliant. Waste is a capital offense.
- **Military Doctrine:** High-thrust, light-hull interceptors heavily integrated with autonomous AI drone wings (`SD-4 Sentry`). Because human life is precious in the Belt, VBC pilots let silicon drones take the brunt of initial enemy passes.

## 3. Vanderbilt-Astra Conglomerate (VAC)
- **Territory:** Private corporate orbital yards, deep-space research outposts, and jump-gate hubs.
- **Culture:** Hyper-capitalist mega-corporation holding patents on capital ship Jump Drives and advanced energy shielding.
- **Military Doctrine:** Sleek, high-precision laser platforms (`LS-2` beam systems), high-end electronic warfare, and heavily paid mercenary squadrons protecting corporate assets.
